## 2. Keep the case study idea, but move the large trajectory figure to the appendix

I would **not remove the tool-use case study concept**, because it is one of the intuitively strongest parts of the paper. It directly motivates why intra-trajectory credit is useful: the model makes an error, receives a correct tool result, and repairs its answer.

Your current Section 7.5 already summarizes the important observation quite efficiently:

> incorrect boxed answer → tool returns 9 → model repairs answer → early segment receives negative credit and later repair receives positive credit. 

But page 10 then spends roughly half a page displaying the entire raw trajectory again as Figure 4. 

I would therefore keep **Section 7.5 in the main text**, but reduce it to something like:

> **Tool-use case study.** A representative Qwen2.5-7B trajectory illustrates the advantage of segment-level credit. The model initially derives the correct equation but outputs an incorrect answer before incorporating the Python result. After observing the tool response \(9\), it repairs the solution. TreeMAPPO assigns weak negative credit to the erroneous pre-tool span and positive credit to the post-tool repair (\(-0.114,+0.222,+0.635,+0.635,+0.576\)). The complete colored trajectory is shown in Appendix G.

That is enough for the argument.

Move Figure 4 beside the existing qualitative examples in Appendix G. Since Appendix G already contains considerably more detailed trajectory analysis, this actually makes the organization cleaner. 

**Saving: another ~0.5 page.**

At this point, you're already almost one page down.

# 3. Merge Sections 8 “Analysis” and 9 “Discussion”

This is probably the **largest textual redundancy** in the current paper.

Section 8 says:

* tree methods trade inference budget for fine-grained credit;
* segmented credit seems particularly useful with tools;
* method effectiveness depends on whether the tree exposes meaningful contrast;
* different branching/credit schemes work in different circumstances. 

Then Section 9 says again:

* TreeMAPPO turns sparse outcomes into dense supervision;
* benefits depend on foundation model/tool context;
* Qwen3 tool is strongest;
* Qwen2.5/Mistral illustrate limitations;
* branching/training/tool-use design choices matter. 

And page 11 continues with four bullets reiterating branching, clipped token-level training, tool/no-tool differences, and forced-token branching. 

These ideas are useful, but they do not need **two full sections plus bullets**.

I would create:

### 8 Discussion

with only **three paragraphs**:

**Paragraph 1 — empirical interpretation**
TreeMAPPO helps when local branches expose meaningful correctness contrast; otherwise the extra granularity gives little benefit.

**Paragraph 2 — why tools matter**
Tool trajectories naturally contain error → feedback → repair transitions, which explains the especially strong Qwen3 tool result and motivates fine-grained credit.

**Paragraph 3 — practical trade-off**
TreeMAPPO spends more rollout compute and introduces variable-advantage optimization, so it should be viewed as complementary to GRPO rather than universally superior.

The four design-choice bullets currently at the beginning of page 11 are mostly repetitions of Method §4.4–4.6 and can simply disappear.

That should turn roughly **1.3 pages of Analysis + Discussion** into perhaps **0.65–0.75 page**.

**Saving: ~0.5 page**, possibly more.

# 4. Related Work is currently too detailed for a 9-page ICLR paper

Your Related Work occupies most of pages 2–4. It is thorough, but at the moment it reads closer to a literature survey than what the paper needs.

For example, the paper individually explains PPO, GRPO, process supervision, Math-Shepherd, Self-Explore, PRIME, PURE, TEMPO, SPO, TreeRL, TreeRPO, TreePO, TreeAdv, Tree-OPO, GRPO-\(\lambda\), OAR, OPPO, GiGPO, APPO, PAL, PoT, ReAct, and Toolformer.  

You don't need to remove the citations. You can **group the papers by family instead of explaining each one**.

For example, the process-supervision paragraph could go from ~9 lines:

> Process supervision and verifier-based credit... Let’s Verify Step by Step... Math-Shepherd... Self-Explore... PRIME... PURE...

to ~3–4 lines:

> **Process-level supervision.** Prior work densifies outcome supervision using human or automatically constructed step labels, learned process verifiers, error localization, or implicit process rewards (Lightman et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2024; Hwang et al., 2024; Cui et al., 2025; Cheng et al., 2025). TreeMAPPO instead derives local credit solely from terminal correctness across deliberately branched policy trajectories.

The same can be done with the tree-based paragraph.

I'd retain the most detail for the papers reviewers are most likely to compare directly against you:

**TEMPO, TreeRPO, TreeRL, TreePO/TreeAdv, APPO**, perhaps SPO.

And compress:

* general PPO/GRPO background;
* process reward-model literature;
* PAL / PoT / ReAct / Toolformer;
* the flat token-credit methods into one sentence.

The final **“Tool-augmented mathematical reasoning and benchmarks”** paragraph is particularly movable/condensable because much of it provides general tool-use context rather than distinguishing TreeMAPPO. 

I think Related Work can go from roughly **1.7 pages to ~1.2 pages** without sacrificing citation coverage.

**Saving: ~0.4–0.5 page.**

# What I would **not** cut

I would protect four things quite strongly.

**First, keep Figure 1.** It is your primary overview and earns its space. The paper introduces several nonstandard components—trunks, token-level branching, MAP credit, flattening—and Figure 1 makes that pipeline legible. 

**Second, keep the MAP formulation, Eqs. 1–5.** This is probably the most distinctive mathematical contribution. 

**Third, keep the branching-score description and forced-token mechanism.** This separates your method from just “construct a tree and assign credit.” 

**Fourth, keep Table 1 in the main text.** It communicates the most important empirical message—including the fact that the method is not universally superior—which makes the paper more credible. 

I would also **keep one sentence or short paragraph about the case study**, because the tool-feedback → repair example provides perhaps the clearest conceptual justification for TreeMAPPO. Just don't spend a full page across Figures 3 and 4 demonstrating it.
